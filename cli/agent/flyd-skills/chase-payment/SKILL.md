# Chase a payment

The goal is money in his account, not a well-worded email. Every draft ends with a date.

1. Establish the facts first, from memory and his project notes: who owes, how much, for what, since when, and who holds the budget now. If the person who agreed the deal has left, write to whoever owns the budget today and give them the history in two lines. Never write to someone who can no longer pay.
2. Pick the rung that fits how late it is:
   - Under 2 weeks late: a friendly nudge and the invoice again.
   - 2 to 6 weeks: firm. Name the amount, the original due date, and a specific pay-by date about 7 days out, and ask for a reply confirming it.
   - Over 6 weeks, or no reply to a firm one: a formal demand with the amount, the dates, the pay-by date, and what happens next (late fee as agreed, pausing further work, small claims). Only threaten what he would actually do.
3. Write it ready to send: real names, amounts and dates computed from today. Use a `[placeholder]` only for what isn't known, and list those placeholders under the draft.
4. Keep it under 150 words, plain and warm-firm. No apology, no "just checking in", no long history.
5. After the draft, offer one follow-up: a reminder on the pay-by date to check whether it landed.
