# Flyd conversation view (`flyd view`)

Reference for the calm conversation view. Product authority: [personal-intelligence PRD](flyd-personal-intelligence-prd.md). Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 2026-10-25; the code under `cli/src/conversation-view/` is the source of truth where this drifts.

## Hosting

- `flyd view [--session id] [--project dir]` serves the view on `127.0.0.1:4818` (`cli/src/conversation-view/`).
- Flyd.app hosts it natively: menu bar "Conversation…" or ⌃⌥⌘F opens a WKWebView window (`mac-adapter/Sources/Conversation/`).
- The app supervises `node --import tsx src/entry.ts view` from Core's `cli/` (`FLYD_VIEW_CLI_DIR` overrides; log `~/.flyd/overlay/conversation-view.log`; `--conversation-selftest` checks the page in-app).
- The view never writes transcripts.

## What it shows

- Only George's words and final replies, followed live.
- v1 source is firstmate's Claude Code transcripts (`ClaudeCodeTranscriptSource`); Flyd chat plugs in as another `ConversationSource`.
- Under the message box: context % from the transcript's own usage (not in Flyd's window, where it is firstmate's machinery) and plan limits from `quota-axi --provider claude` (read-only, no Keychain prompt).
- firstmate's transcript lines show only as muted "update" relays (routine ones hidden), and all firstmate text is put in Flyd's voice ("sir", never "Captain"; `flyd-voice.ts`).
- Most messages are just messages. A chat reply is drawn as a card only when it carries finished work set before rendering (`card-intent.ts`): the page its turn changed, or a screenshot of work its relay reports landed. A screenshot the turn only looked at, links, lists, headings or a question in a reply never make it a card; a question's answer buttons ride under the message, or under the result card when it carries one.
- "taste" in the header opens `/taste` (see [flyd-taste.md](flyd-taste.md)).
- `/api/status` feeds the notch island (working / a failed line / reply / needs you). Clicking the island opens the window at the item its reply is about; a Core notification (`/notify`, shown on the island) is clickable too and opens its `item`, or what needs him first (`"needs"`) for a decision or blocker. The page takes these links as `window.flydOpen(id)` or `?job=<id>` and opens the item in place in the timeline (`page/script-timeline.ts`).
- Placement is decided once in Core (`placement.ts`): every item sits in Needs you (his move), Moving (Flyd's move) or Done, with one set of state words (`STATE_WORDS`). The right column, the timeline and the island all read that answer.
- The middle column is one lasting conversation, the timeline (`page/script-timeline.ts`): the page joins the newest window conversation rather than starting a fresh one. What needs him, work under way and what finished today arrive in it as Flyd's own messages among his words. The conversation is append-only: a job's message is placed once, at its own record's time (`Job.toldAt`, which a conversation naming its project never moves) when it was on the board as the page came, or at the bottom when it arrives later, and it never moves after that; its place is kept in the browser so a reload puts it back. Project threads are a filtered view and never move anything in the main chat. The stream draws the newest 150 exchanges (`STREAM_EXCHANGES`, server.ts); scrolling to the top asks `POST /api/earlier` for 100 more, put in above what he is reading without moving him. The model's context is separate: the newest 4 exchanges whole plus a short recap of up to 12 earlier ones (`earlierRecap`, flyd-desk.ts). Each message has Reply (sets the one message box's reply chip: the quoted title on one line at the top of the box, inside its border, with a ✕ to dismiss; a leading "Flyd:" is dropped from the quote) and Open (the job unfolds in place under its message). A finished-work card instead has exactly Reply and OK: it keeps its header, title, body and single picture, never unfolds, and OK dismisses it (the same path as the card dismiss) and clears it from the timeline. Earlier conversations fold away at the top of the timeline. There is no Today feed and no Artefact (TV) view; the Conversation tab is home.
- The **News** tab says whether the news is working (`newsStatus` in `activity.ts`): feeds on or off, when today's edition came in and when the morning brief was ready, with a warning when the brief has not run by 08:00 or the news writer's model calls are failing (from scout's `run-state.json` and the agenda log). Under that it shows the latest editions' stories (filler sources Giphy, Pexels and Google Trends left out) beside a weather card from `weather.ts` (`/api/weather`, token-gated; needs `~/.flyd/location.json` or `FLYD_WEATHER_LOCATION`). On News a story is talked about only once he picks it with the plain "Chat about this" link under its source: the message box then names it in a removable chip (✕ returns the box to the conversation), and pointing at or scrolling past other stories never changes it. The box has no project picker on News; with no story picked its placeholder names where the message goes instead. His question and Flyd's answer sit under that story, in its article conversation. There is no horoscope source yet.
- The **Week** tab (`week.ts`, `page/script-week.ts`, `/api/week`, token-gated) shows eight days, hour by hour: a strip per day, coloured Flyd, Logan and DIR (always all three in the key, even in a quiet week) and one neutral for everything else, with the day picked told underneath. Nothing new is tracked: the projects are firstmate's registry (`data/projects.md`, each line's live copy) and the work is each live copy's git history (every branch, tag and remote, stashes and merges left out, a change counted once in the local hour of its author time). The day's story is derived from the same changes: the busiest project's most telling change (a feature over a fix over the rest) leads, then one line for each of the next three projects. Pointing at a block (hover, a tap, or up/down on a focused day; left/right step days) tells that block in the story's place, in the same type, with no card or popup: the project as the headline, the day, the whole hours the block spans and the count of changes, then the latest five in plain words with their PR where the subject names one and a closing "and N earlier" when there are more, as one list beside the headline (two columns on a desktop or tablet, stacked on a phone) so a block stays one row (`work` on each day). Letting go (or a tap elsewhere) brings the day's story back. The headline and story keep the tallest day's or block's height at the width shown, so picking another never moves what sits under the headline or the strips above. Read at most every ten minutes. On a desktop or tablet the right column steps aside, as on News, and the heading, strips and story sit as one group centred in the height, the strips growing with the room.
- Fleet reads request all in-flight rows (`--all-in-flight`). Working, paused, queued and finished rows remain visible even when the executive does not notify about them. Each running worker has its own line in the Now column. Queued work is Next, not Working; accepted or working non-FirstMate runs that have not moved for a day read Waiting. Finished work can be ready without a merged PR. Paused, parked and blocked workers read as Paused; grouped cards show a paused count when needed. Finished rows and runs are Ready for you for a day, then done. An unanswered ask stays in Needs you however old it is (an old call only stops ringing him), and one whose FirstMate task closed or that George called old (`closedBy`, `command/run-closure.ts`) never asks again. When George says a card is old, Flyd's `retire_card` tool retires it (`retire.ts`, `retired-cards.ts`), settles Flyd's own run or lab and tells FirstMate to close the hold with his words; Flyd never promises to stop raising something without it.
- Finished work is told to George once (`intelligence/executive/finished-check.ts`, `runtime/delivered.ts`). Flyd checks it itself: a named PR's merge state through `gh`, a named commit against the default branch, otherwise the task's own done report. Then it says one line per report with the link, naming the work by the change's own title, else the task's title, else his own words quoted unchanged as his request ("Your request “…” is done"; words naming backstage, and outcomes Flyd wrote for him (a slash command's or attachment's stand-in, a chat tool's brief, a follow-up), are never quoted, the bare line is said instead, or "…is finished; I haven't checked it yet" when nothing could be checked), never his request itself said to be fixed. A commit checked and not yet on the default branch is "waiting to go in". A result that still carries open questions or risks goes through the ordinary decision path instead. Each report is claimed as a `flyd.delivered` event before it is said, so no two processes both say it and a restart never repeats it; each report is keyed only by its own work. Core is the one announcer of finished work in every domain (FirstMate, Librarian, design): it tells each outcome of a run once, when the staff's stable-outcome reply arrives, under `work:<run id>:<outcome time>` (`command/scheduler.ts` `notifyRun`; the run's `report` record says whether he saw it, see `flyd-command-architecture-prd.md`). The window never rings for fleet rows; work George started with FirstMate outside Flyd is FirstMate's to report. Flyd's own (backstage) runs, and runs whose reply shows under his message in the conversation the window has open, are recorded as handled (outcome `kept`) instead of rung. A report no adapter took is left owed and a run is not marked told; every command tick (from Core start) sweeps owed reports from the last day and says them again, each wait twice the last (5 minutes first), giving up after six more tries; these reports, already told once by their own key, skip the once-a-day line repeat filter, so two runs whose lines read alike are each told. One cut off mid-send is said once more. Core's first command tick after this shipped records every run already finished as told without ringing (once ever, `seed:finished.v1`). Work George started from the window is announced the same way unless the window is open on that conversation (`conversation-view/window-presence.ts`).
- Open domain decisions can be answered on their Jobs cards. Runs in the done phase cannot accept an answer there.
- An open job can always be replied to (`page/script-front.ts`, `frontReplyButton`; `quote` in `page/script-artefact-chat.ts`): Reply above its links takes him to the job's own box, and each picture (the one on show, or each lab version) carries its own Reply that fills the box with `On "<picture name>": ` and attaches that picture, ready for his words. Nothing is sent until he sends it, and it goes where the job's box always goes (its run, its decision, or the job itself). A picture he taps still opens full size.
- A Needs you card is read at a glance: the picture, what he must do (else what changed, else what comes next) in full, then the project and time, and an action line only for a concrete next step ("2 of 6 decided", Flyd's recommended pick). A Needs you card shows only the pictures and links its own question carried, never another item's on the same project; other cards lead with their own, then the project's. Each shows once, and a repeated label is told apart by number ("Open board · 2"). A Needs you decision that names a few answers (a one-question fleet call's options, a yes-or-no or option question, or Flyd's own "Say merge…" / "Say yes … or tell me to …" wording; `namedAnswers` in `choices.ts`) shows them as buttons on the card instead of that line, the recommended one first; the open job shows the same buttons over its box ("Or say something else…"), and both send through the same chat. A tap counts only for the question it answered, so a later question on the same job gets fresh buttons. Open questions, several questions at once and pick-any lists get no buttons on the card. A decision (fleet hold or run ask) carrying several numbered questions ("1) … 2) …", `holdDecisions` in `artefact.ts`) shows one card per question in the open job, each with its own answer buttons and words (an open "anything beyond …?" offers "No, that's all"); each answer goes back tagged with its question and only that card is marked answered. A numbered list of options for one question stays one decision with buttons. The full digest stays in the open job; its Timeline sits behind a small "Timeline" link, closed by default, each entry shown as its whole sentence.
- A Jobs card built from the staff's own words (a fleet item's status line or a run's brief; a card that puts a question to George in plain words keeps its question and answers as asked, and only its title is said plainly) never shows them: Flyd's quick model says its title and line once as a plain outcome (`card-words.ts`, cached in `~/.flyd/card-words.json` by raw line, a call's by its question identity so a reworded call is not read again, and a Needs you line that asks nothing is read as a notice that never invents a question; scheduled by the server, never while drawing; a reading that still carries hashes, file names, note numbers, branch/main, land, merge, satisfied, PR, checks, worker, build, deploy, tests or ship words is refused, and a line the model fails on three times is not asked again). A decision run with no question of its own, or one asked in developer words, is said the same way, so Needs you never shows the staff's line. Until then, or when the model fails, the card reads a plain line for where it stands that says what a card needing George needs ("<Project>: ready for you to look over", "needs your answer", "work is under way", "held up for now", "waiting for now" or "finished"); the open job's Timeline shows the same plain words, and every run's Timeline entry is said the same way, never as the raw line. Board placeholders ("Under way", "Queued") are never sent to the model. What the card finally shows is checked once more for developer words and banned phrases and said plainly if any remain, and its answer buttons read in George's words too ("Merge" reads "Go ahead", "Wait for checks" reads "Wait") while still sending the answer as named; answers that cannot be said plainly are left to his own words. A question asked in developer words shows no buttons until its stored reading puts to him the question they answer. If the model gives up on it, the question is shown exactly as asked, with its buttons: a rare developer word beats a garbled question. Finished work's state reads "Ready for you", and a project's bare kind ("product") is not shown.
- A card naming a Liesel board (`127.0.0.1:4747/board/<key>`) shows a picture of that board as it stands (`board-shots.ts`, `/api/board-shot`, token-gated): Flyd opens the existing board in its own headless Chrome (throwaway profile), keeps one screen in `~/.flyd/view/board-shots`, retakes it once 30 minutes old, and serves only boards a card names (a fleet card, or a Jobs run that needs him or is under way naming a board in its artifacts or evidence; finished runs are never pictured). The board is never created or changed. A board named only in an update's words ("reply posted on http://127.0.0.1:4747/board/…") counts too, and a screenshot the worker's last status line names by path leads the card ahead of the board picture. Board links in a card's words open on the phone's address: `FLYD_LIESEL_URL`, default `http://macbook-pro-3.tail46029c.ts.net:4747`. A board's picture and button say which board by the name in its `board.json` ("DIR start control board", "Open DIR start control board"; `BoardNames` in `liesel.ts`), else the picture reads "Board for <work>" and the button "Open board". A card's board button goes through Flyd's `/open-board/<key>` (`liesel.ts`, `open-board-page.ts`): a "One moment, opening the board" loader asks Liesel for the board (token-gated `/api/open-board`), starts Liesel when nothing answers on 4747 (its own `bin/liesel-server.js server --port 4747 --also-listen 100.78.155.0` from `FLYD_LIESEL_DIR`, default `~/Documents/easel`, detached, logging to `~/.easel/server.log`; one start shared by every waiting page, never a second Liesel), waits until it serves the board, then opens it (loopback on the Mac, the public origin elsewhere). A board Liesel answers 404 for is said to no longer exist. A card on a project that keeps a board in `<project>/.liesel/board.json` (read from `~/.easel/state.json`, ended boards skipped) links that board when it names none, beside any Lavish review link it already has.
- Finished work gets a card when it has anything to see or open: pictures, its own link, a board, or a running project URL; a picture is never required (`landedRows` in `artefact.ts`). A board URL in the task's own data folder (`.md`/`.txt`/`.json`) links that board. A finished card with no PR or board link of its own opens its project where it runs now, from `~/.flyd/project-urls.json` (`{ "logan": "http://127.0.0.1:3002" }`, keyed by project name; `project-urls.ts`); a loopback link reads "Open".
- His own to-dos sit in the right column's Needs you, under the calls, and count toward it; there is no Todo tab or add field (Flyd adds them through the `todos` tool). His items are the confirmed to-dos (`confirmed_todos` in the work index, the list briefings and the `todos` tool read), so an item ticked here is the same item everywhere. Flyd's asks already have their card in Needs you, so the list keeps only rows with no job of their own; failed agenda runs from the last week that have not since run cleanly and whose item has not been cancelled are listed too. A ticked item leaves the list. Rows show their words verbatim.
- An answer is never refused because its question went stale. The server reads the fleet afresh (or the run's current state) and compares the full, case-sensitive question text, not the shortened display copy. The same question under a new id still takes the answer. If the question has closed or changed, or the fleet can't be read, his words and attachments reach the same job as a normal message, so they never decide a different question. If an answer is recorded but its picture fails, the answer stands and only the picture can be retried.
- A card's chat box never makes him wait on that read. Core accepts each note at once (held in memory, never written to disk) and routes the card's notes one at a time in the order he sent them; the card swaps the accepted id for the real one when routing ends. Send is never blocked by an earlier note: later notes show "Waiting to send". A note that fails stays in the thread as "Not delivered" with Try again, which resends it under the same client id so it is never routed twice. A note is only ever sent from the card copy he typed it in: a reload or another copy of the card never sends it again.

## Project threads

- The right column has three tabs: **Now**, the home sidebar (`page/sidebar-lib.ts` holds its content rules, `page/script-front.ts` draws it): Needs you as cards; **Now**, one line a job for work under way and then what finished lately (the project, a small static dot whose rollover says where it stands and since when, and one whole live line of what is happening, written to fit and never cut off with an ellipsis; a line that changes rises into place); Next, a closed count of queued, paused and held-up work; and **For this chat**, inspirations that follow a few terms from his newest words (`page/sidebar-lib.ts` `sideTerms`). Once the chat settles (2 seconds after the terms change; at once on first load) the page says "Looking for <terms>…" over shimmer placeholders and asks `GET /api/inspirations?q=<terms>` (token-gated; `inspirations.ts`) for one quick Evidence Engine lookup; Scout's stories and Made artefacts already on the page that match show at once, and the Engine's titled public links arrive after, one after another. **Find more** looks again; the same terms redrawn never look again. What it finds is evidence shown to him, never written into memory. A picture shows only when the item flags it as meant to be seen (a news story whose picture is the story), never because a screenshot exists. Cards open their link; there is no Add to canvas action yet, because the work canvas (`work-canvas.ts`) is a read-only projection with no add path. Every Now line opens its item in place in the timeline (a project board view is not built). On a phone the Now tab folds into one live line under the tabs (what needs him first, else what is moving, else "Nothing is under way right now."), always shown once the board is read; a tap opens the sidebar as a sheet with a Close button. Held-up work whose plain reading asks for his call is placed in Needs you; a finished card with only "finished" to say, or a notice overtaken by newer work on its project, is shown nowhere (`bare`), **Projects** and **Made** (the artefacts). Its **Projects** tab is a contents page, newest first, with a project he has successfully commented on below the projects he has not yet gone through: each project's name and time, his words (whole when short, else their first sentence) and Flyd's latest line in one sentence, with a dot on what he has not read. A box above the list shows the newest project that has a picture (the thread's newest image or kept screenshot), unless it is already open or he has commented on it, and that project leaves the list so each exchange is shown once; **All conversations** and **Other** sit as quiet links under the list. Picking one opens its thread in the middle, "Back to the conversation" leaves it. What he has read is his browser's own (`localStorage`), counted from his first look, so history never arrives as unread.
- **Machines**, the header's tab after Conversation, News and Week (`machines.ts`, `machine-words.ts`, `page/script-machines.ts`, `page/machine-style.ts`), is a whole screen: one row per machine, the computer Flyd runs on first, each its name as a heavy headline over one plain sentence from Core about what matters there (memory held by one app and full swap, leftover test browsers, a full disk, why it is offline, or "All well") and at most one dark button. The local machine is read with `sh`; the others with one fixed read-only script over `ssh <alias>` in batch mode, each on its own timeout so a dead machine only marks its own row; the script sums `ps` per program on the machine, so the reading stays small however busy it is; every 30 minutes, after a good reading, a separate read asks Claude Code and Codex for `--version`, so a slow or stuck tool never marks a machine offline. The page reads only while the screen is up (every 30 seconds) and Core reads a machine at most every 20 seconds. The list lives in `~/.flyd/machines.json` as names and aliases only, starting from the PC (`pc-firstmate`) alone until the first machine is added; a file Flyd cannot fully read is never saved over, and adding says so. Keys, users and ports stay in the SSH config. Readings stay in memory. The right column, on the same paper, holds the tool versions in one sentence with one button, then from `machine-setup.ts` which model does which job in one sentence (`lib/model-setup.ts` and the LIVE model), this month's Claude spend (`~/.flyd/claude-spend.json`) and what each coding plan has left from Firstmate's `quota-axi` (read-only, at most every 5 minutes). The tool-versions sentence says which machines run an older Claude Code or Codex than the newest npm release (read at most every 6 hours). A button sends Core's words to Flyd in the conversation, leaving the message box as it is (a short line says so when it could not be sent); one that changes something (closing processes, updating tools) asks George on the page first. Reading the screen takes the token.
- `project-threads.ts` reads Flyd's conversations and firstmate's session transcripts in the background, then refreshes changed or active sessions. `readHistory` reads a long transcript in pieces. A thread is a session to the rest of the view (`project:dir`), capped at its newest 200 messages. firstmate's reports that answer nothing he said pass the same check a relay passes in the stream, or stay out of the threads; a reply to him is never kept back.
- State of play (`lib/state-of-play.ts`): a firstmate round-up (project headings with status points: landed, started, under way, needs you, stuck) is neither a George decision nor a verified result, so the executive gate keeps it backstage. The threads' firstmate feed reads it instead and keeps each project's newest report in `~/.flyd/view/state-of-play.json` (36 h current) as one Flyd line per project. What landed or needs him rings once, a notification per project (`deliverNotificationLine`); a project's line shown to him whole counts as told, and the next briefing (`composeMorning`) carries the rest he has not heard (held back, shortened or only under way), which count as told only once a chat surface prints them (greeting, intro or `/brief`, via `markPlayShown`); Flyd's chat prompt and staff read carry it as staff evidence, never verified fact or memory.
- Tags (`project-tags.ts`): names first, from firstmate's `data/projects.md`, Flyd's project list and the older names in `KNOWN`, in his words and firstmate's, task ids and repo URLs included. A turn (what he said, what came back) that names nothing carries on the project of the talk within 20 minutes of it, when that is one project. A message naming several projects is in each; a thread leaves out the paragraphs and points that name only others. A message naming more than four is treated as a fleet round-up and has no project tag.
- A turn whose words name no project is placed from its metadata first, with no model call: the project of the run its note opened (`request.project` name or code root, matched against the projects registry), else the repo of a known firstmate task id his words name (an exact `state/<id>.meta` record, read to its `project=` repo), else the project whose own conversation (`window:project-<slug>`) it was said in. A word that only starts with a registry slug (`site-wide`) is not a task id and places nothing. Only his free lines left after that, and after the talk around them, go to Flyd's cheap model (`project-tagger.ts`): one call per sweep, at most 25 lines cut short, spaced 15 minutes apart. Every line in a batch is settled by that one call; what the model skips, names wrongly or a failed call leaves goes under **Other** and is never asked again. Answers are kept by hash in `~/.flyd/view/project-tags.json`. Nothing is sent to firstmate for tagging.
- His words in a thread go to that project's own conversation (`window:project-dir`), or to the live session when the source keeps none, led by `About the project "DIR"` and the last few lines of the thread (never shown to him); a message he points elsewhere with **To:** goes without that lead, so a follow-up means something there. A slash command goes as typed.

## The message box is Flyd's

- One model call routes each message (`flyd-desk.ts`).
- Above the box, a project picker (no "To:" label; reads "Project" when none is chosen) says where a message goes (`ChatScope`, `runtime/chat-scope.ts`): general chat unless he is in a project's thread or picks a project from its menu. It is sent with the message and goes exactly where it shows; Flyd's turn reads it as one line, so earlier talk about a project never makes a general message that project's.
- Shift+Up recalls his earlier messages, newest first, and Shift+Down walks back to the draft; plain Up and Down only move the caret.
- General questions get Flyd's own assistant turn (kept in `~/.flyd/view/asks`). A turn that fails in passing is asked again once by Flyd itself, and one cut off by a restart is asked again once at startup (only asks older than the process, within an hour); George is never told to send it again. A turn that already ran a side-effecting tool (`acted`) is never replayed and shows the plain failure (`flyd-desk.ts`).
- Software/fleet work, slash commands and attached files go to firstmate's own `bin/fm-inbox.sh note`, each as a `desk-…` domain run through the command layer's FirstMate transport (his words, done_when, return contract; settled from receipts, transcript matching only a fallback). Only on firstmate's conversation.
- A screenshot goes with his words to whichever of them answers; Flyd reads it through a vision description kept with the ask.
- The **+** beside the box picks images and documents (the same types and limits as a drop or paste). In Flyd's own window documents go to Flyd's turn: saved under `~/.flyd/view/asks/files/<id>/`, read as text (`document-text.ts`: plain text as is, Word/RTF/OpenDocument via `textutil`, PDF via `pdftotext` when installed) and fenced in the prompt as outside content, so the turn is tainted like one that has read the web. The retrieval query, route and answer shape use his own words only; follow-ups replay an excerpt of each document.
- firstmate home is `~/Documents/firstmate` (`FLYD_FIRSTMATE_HOME` overrides; never the real one under vitest — `cli/src/lib/firstmate-home.ts`).
- "/" at the start of the box lists Claude Code's skills and commands (`commands.ts`, `/api/commands`); a sent command carries a hidden marker telling firstmate to run it. Each project with a thread is listed too as its shortcut ("/flyd", the thread's own slug, described by its live line): picking it, or sending exactly "/flyd", opens that project's thread instead of sending anything; a skill of the same name still runs as typed.
- Pasted images: transcript ones are read back by line byte offset (`/api/image`); box ones are saved to firstmate's `data/inbox-images` and named in the note as `[image: /path]`.
- After push-to-talk speech stops, the box shows the Dynamic Island's spoke loader while the words are transcribed and for as long as the island shows Flyd working on the reply (`page/script-composer.ts`); it mirrors the island's working state (`refreshWorking` in `page/script-messages.ts`) and the voice events, with no timer of its own, and clears on a failed or cancelled recording, a send that did not go, or a reply shown elsewhere.
- Composer typing suggestions (Tab accepts, Escape dismisses, ↹ toggles): [flyd-composer-predictions.md](flyd-composer-predictions.md).

## Answers under a question

- Grounded visual answers can supply facts through `describe_answer` during the normal assistant turn (`runtime/conversation-responder.ts`), before the final takeaway. Core validates source quotes/URLs against the turn's actual evidence and applies the same grounding/action-claim checks as prose. The tool follows the surface's offered tool set and refusals; it is absent from mobile, article and delegated-work turns and cannot execute actions.
- The active answer path is `SemanticObject → CommunicationIntent → ManifestationPlan → Renderer` (`conversation-view/communication.ts`, `commands/view.ts`). An investigation explaining a cause produces a cause/expected/actual/evidence/fix composition; an investigation comparing fixes produces a criteria table, recommendation and evidence. Other findings and uncertainty remain visible. Kind never selects a layout. Ordinary conversation stays prose; legacy stored answer forms still render.
