# Flyd conversation view (`flyd view`)

Reference for the calm conversation view. Product authority: [personal-intelligence PRD](flyd-personal-intelligence-prd.md). Moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 2026-10-25; the code under `cli/src/conversation-view/` is the source of truth where this drifts.

## Hosting

- `flyd view [--session id] [--project dir]` serves the view on `127.0.0.1:4818` (`cli/src/conversation-view/`).
- Flyd.app hosts it natively: menu bar "Conversation…" or ⌃⌥⌘F opens a WKWebView window (`mac-adapter/Sources/Conversation/`).
- The app supervises `node --import tsx src/entry.ts view` from Core's `cli/` (`FLYD_VIEW_CLI_DIR` overrides; log `~/.flyd/overlay/conversation-view.log`; `--conversation-selftest` checks the page in-app).
- The view never writes transcripts.

## What it shows

- Only George's words and final replies, followed live.
- v1 source is firstmate's Claude Code transcripts (`ClaudeCodeTranscriptSource`); Flyd chat plugs in as another `ConversationSource`.
- Under the message box: context % from the transcript's own usage (not in Flyd's window, where it is firstmate's machinery) and plan limits from `quota-axi --provider claude` (read-only, no Keychain prompt).
- firstmate's transcript lines show only as muted "update" relays (routine ones hidden), and all firstmate text is put in Flyd's voice ("sir", never "Captain"; `flyd-voice.ts`).
- "taste" in the header opens `/taste` (see [flyd-taste.md](flyd-taste.md)).
- `/api/status` feeds the notch island (working / a failed line / reply / needs you).
- `live-activity.ts` builds the `live` model sent with each stream update. Its counts can differ from the right column's job cards, which group a project's calls.
- At rest the middle column is **Today** (`page/script-today.ts`): today's events as cards from `update.live` (running and landed work, moving work first with a pulsing dot) plus "You left off on …" for his latest other conversation today, which opens it again. A conversation takes the middle once he writes; the Today tab brings the feed back. On a phone the feed follows the Jobs cards. Above the cards Today says whether the news is working (`newsStatus` in `activity.ts`): feeds on or off, when today's edition came in and when the morning brief was ready, with a warning when the brief has not run by 08:00 or the news writer's model calls are failing (from scout's `run-state.json` and the agenda log).
- The **News** tab shows the latest editions' stories (filler sources Giphy, Pexels and Google Trends left out) beside a weather card from `weather.ts` (`/api/weather`, token-gated; needs `~/.flyd/location.json` or `FLYD_WEATHER_LOCATION`). On News the message box asks about the story in view or picked; his question and Flyd's answer sit under that story, in its article conversation. There is no horoscope source yet.
- The **Week** tab (`week.ts`, `page/script-week.ts`, `/api/week`, token-gated) shows eight days, hour by hour: a strip per day, coloured Flyd, Logan and DIR (always all three in the key, even in a quiet week) and one neutral for everything else, with the day picked told underneath. Nothing new is tracked: the projects are firstmate's registry (`data/projects.md`, each line's live copy) and the work is each live copy's git history (every branch, tag and remote, stashes and merges left out, a change counted once in the local hour of its author time). The day's story is derived from the same changes: the busiest project's most telling change (a feature over a fix over the rest) leads, then one line for each of the next three projects. Pointing at a block (hover, a tap, or up/down on a focused day; left/right step days) opens a card beside its strip: the project, the whole hours the block spans, the count of changes and the latest five in plain words with their PR where the subject names one (`work` on each day). The card stays on screen and above the message box, flips sides at the edges and never covers its block. Read at most every ten minutes. On a desktop or tablet the right column steps aside, as on News, and the heading, strips and story sit as one group centred in the height, the strips growing with the room.
- Fleet reads request all in-flight rows (`--all-in-flight`). Working, paused, queued and finished rows remain visible even when the executive does not notify about them. Each running worker has its own Jobs card. Queued work is not Working; accepted or working non-FirstMate runs that have not moved for a day are Waiting. Finished work can be ready without a merged PR. Paused, parked and blocked workers read as Paused; grouped cards show a paused count when needed.
- Finished work is told to George once (`intelligence/executive/finished-check.ts`, `runtime/delivered.ts`). Flyd checks it itself: a named PR's merge state through `gh`, a named commit against the default branch, otherwise the task's own done report. Then it says one line per report with the link, naming the work by the change's own title, else the task's title, else his own words quoted unchanged as his request ("Your request “…” is done"; words naming backstage, and outcomes Flyd wrote for him (a slash command's or attachment's stand-in, a chat tool's brief, a follow-up), are never quoted, the bare line is said instead, or "…is finished; I haven't checked it yet" when nothing could be checked), never his request itself said to be fixed. A commit checked and not yet on the default branch is "waiting to go in". A result that still carries open questions or risks goes through the ordinary decision path instead. Each report is claimed as a `flyd.delivered` event before it is said, so no two processes both say it and a restart never repeats it; each report is keyed only by its own work. Core is the one announcer of finished work in every domain (FirstMate, Librarian, design): it tells each outcome of a run once, when the staff's stable-outcome reply arrives, under `work:<run id>:<outcome time>` (`command/scheduler.ts` `notifyRun`; the run's `report` record says whether he saw it, see `flyd-command-architecture-prd.md`). The window never rings for fleet rows; work George started with FirstMate outside Flyd is FirstMate's to report. Flyd's own (backstage) runs, and runs whose reply shows under his message in the conversation the window has open, are recorded as handled (outcome `kept`) instead of rung. A report no adapter took is left owed and a run is not marked told; every command tick (from Core start) sweeps owed reports from the last day and says them again, each wait twice the last (5 minutes first), giving up after six more tries; these reports, already told once by their own key, skip the once-a-day line repeat filter, so two runs whose lines read alike are each told. One cut off mid-send is said once more. Core's first command tick after this shipped records every run already finished as told without ringing (once ever, `seed:finished.v1`). Work George started from the window is announced the same way unless the window is open on that conversation (`conversation-view/window-presence.ts`).
- Open domain decisions can be answered on their Jobs cards. Runs in the done phase cannot accept an answer there.
- A Jobs card is read at a glance: the project and time along the top, the picture (Working cards are words only, with a pulsing dot after the project), what he must do (else what changed, else what comes next) in full, and an action line only for a concrete next step ("2 of 6 decided", Flyd's recommended pick). A Needs you decision that names a few answers (a one-question fleet call's options, a yes-or-no or option question, or Flyd's own "Say merge…" / "Say yes … or tell me to …" wording; `namedAnswers` in `choices.ts`) shows them as buttons on the card instead of that line, the recommended one first; the open job shows the same buttons over its box ("Or say something else…"), and both send through the same chat. A tap counts only for the question it answered, so a later question on the same job gets fresh buttons. Open questions, several questions at once and pick-any lists get no buttons on the card. A decision (fleet hold or run ask) carrying several numbered questions ("1) … 2) …", `holdDecisions` in `artefact.ts`) shows one card per question in the open job, each with its own answer buttons and words (an open "anything beyond …?" offers "No, that's all"); each answer goes back tagged with its question and only that card is marked answered. A numbered list of options for one question stays one decision with buttons. The full digest stays in the open job; its Timeline sits behind a small "Timeline" link, closed by default, each entry shown as its whole sentence.
- A Jobs card built from the staff's own words (a fleet item's status line or a run's brief; a card that puts a question to George keeps its question and answers as asked, and only its title is said plainly) never shows them: Flyd's quick model says its title and line once as a plain outcome (`card-words.ts`, cached in `~/.flyd/card-words.json` by raw line, scheduled by the server, never while drawing; a reading that still carries hashes, file names, note numbers, branch/main, land, satisfied or PR words is refused). Until then, or when the model fails, the card reads a plain line for where it stands ("<Project>: an update is ready", "work is under way", "waiting for now" or "finished") with "Open to see details."; the open job's Timeline shows the same plain words, and every run's Timeline entry is said the same way, never as the raw line. A card whose line is itself a question with answers keeps it as asked. Finished work's state reads "Ready for you", and a project's bare kind ("product") is not shown.
- A card naming a Liesel board (`127.0.0.1:4747/board/<key>`) shows a picture of that board as it stands (`board-shots.ts`, `/api/board-shot`, token-gated): Flyd opens the existing board in its own headless Chrome (throwaway profile), keeps one screen in `~/.flyd/view/board-shots`, retakes it once 30 minutes old, and serves only boards a card names. The board is never created or changed. A board named only in an update's words ("reply posted on http://127.0.0.1:4747/board/…") counts too, and a screenshot the worker's last status line names by path leads the card ahead of the board picture. Board links in a card's words open on the phone's address: `FLYD_LIESEL_URL`, default `http://macbook-pro-3.tail46029c.ts.net:4747`. A card's "Open board" button goes through Flyd's `/open-board/<key>` (`liesel.ts`, `open-board-page.ts`): a "One moment, opening the board" loader asks Liesel for the board (token-gated `/api/open-board`), starts Liesel when nothing answers on 4747 (its own `bin/liesel-server.js server --port 4747 --also-listen 100.78.155.0` from `FLYD_LIESEL_DIR`, default `~/Documents/easel`, detached, logging to `~/.easel/server.log`; one start shared by every waiting page, never a second Liesel), waits until it serves the board, then opens it (loopback on the Mac, the public origin elsewhere). A board Liesel answers 404 for is said to no longer exist. A card on a project that keeps a board in `<project>/.liesel/board.json` (read from `~/.easel/state.json`, ended boards skipped) links that board when it names none, and drops any Lavish review link for it.
- The Todo tab lists what he must do himself. His own items are the confirmed to-dos (`confirmed_todos` in the work index, the list briefings and the `todos` tool read), so an item added or ticked here is the same item everywhere. Flyd's asks are read fresh on every draw and never join that list: Needs you jobs, and agenda runs that failed in the last week, have not since run cleanly and whose item has not been cancelled. A ticked item leaves the tab. A ticked ask is kept only as a done row carrying the ask's `source_ref`; it does not answer the decision, which stays on its Jobs card. Rows with no job to open show their words verbatim.
- An answer is never refused because its question went stale. The server reads the fleet afresh (or the run's current state) and compares the full, case-sensitive question text, not the shortened display copy. The same question under a new id still takes the answer. If the question has closed or changed, or the fleet can't be read, his words and attachments reach the same job as a normal message, so they never decide a different question. If an answer is recorded but its picture fails, the answer stands and only the picture can be retried.
- A card's chat box never makes him wait on that read. Core accepts each note at once (held in memory, never written to disk) and routes the card's notes one at a time in the order he sent them; the card swaps the accepted id for the real one when routing ends. Send is never blocked by an earlier note: later notes show "Waiting to send". A note that fails stays in the thread as "Not delivered" with Try again, which resends it under the same client id so it is never routed twice. A note is only ever sent from the card copy he typed it in: a reload or another copy of the card never sends it again.

## Project threads

- The right column's **Projects** tab lists each project's latest exchange (his question, the first line of what came back) with a dot on what he has not read; **All** heads it. Picking one opens its thread in the middle, "Back to the conversation" leaves it. What he has read is his browser's own (`localStorage`), counted from his first look, so history never arrives as unread.
- **Machines**, the header's tab after Today, News and Week (`machines.ts`, `machine-words.ts`, `page/script-machines.ts`, `page/machine-style.ts`), is a whole screen: one row per machine, the computer Flyd runs on first, each its name as a heavy headline over one plain sentence from Core about what matters there (memory held by one app and full swap, leftover test browsers, a full disk, why it is offline, or "All well") and at most one dark button. The local machine is read with `sh`; the others with one fixed read-only script over `ssh <alias>` in batch mode, each on its own timeout so a dead machine only marks its own row; the script sums `ps` per program on the machine, so the reading stays small however busy it is; every 30 minutes, after a good reading, a separate read asks Claude Code and Codex for `--version`, so a slow or stuck tool never marks a machine offline. The page reads only while the screen is up (every 30 seconds) and Core reads a machine at most every 20 seconds. The list lives in `~/.flyd/machines.json` as names and aliases only, starting from the PC (`pc-firstmate`) alone until the first machine is added; a file Flyd cannot fully read is never saved over, and adding says so. Keys, users and ports stay in the SSH config. Readings stay in memory. The right column, on the same paper, holds the tool versions in one sentence with one button, then from `machine-setup.ts` which model does which job in one sentence (`lib/model-setup.ts` and the LIVE model), this month's Claude spend (`~/.flyd/claude-spend.json`) and what each coding plan has left from Firstmate's `quota-axi` (read-only, at most every 5 minutes). The tool-versions sentence says which machines run an older Claude Code or Codex than the newest npm release (read at most every 6 hours). A button sends Core's words to Flyd in the conversation, leaving the message box as it is (a short line says so when it could not be sent); one that changes something (closing processes, updating tools) asks George on the page first. Reading the screen takes the token.
- `project-threads.ts` reads Flyd's conversations and firstmate's session transcripts in the background, then refreshes changed or active sessions. `readHistory` reads a long transcript in pieces. A thread is a session to the rest of the view (`project:dir`), capped at its newest 200 messages. firstmate's reports that answer nothing he said pass the same check a relay passes in the stream, or stay out of the threads; a reply to him is never kept back.
- State of play (`lib/state-of-play.ts`): a firstmate round-up (project headings with status points: landed, started, under way, needs you, stuck) is neither a George decision nor a verified result, so the executive gate keeps it backstage. The threads' firstmate feed reads it instead and keeps each project's newest report in `~/.flyd/view/state-of-play.json` (36 h current) as one Flyd line per project. What landed or needs him rings once, a notification per project (`deliverNotificationLine`); a project's line shown to him whole counts as told, and the next briefing (`composeMorning`) carries the rest he has not heard (held back, shortened or only under way), which count as told only once a chat surface prints them (greeting, intro or `/brief`, via `markPlayShown`); Flyd's chat prompt and staff read carry it as staff evidence, never verified fact or memory.
- Tags (`project-tags.ts`): names first, from firstmate's `data/projects.md`, Flyd's project list and the older names in `KNOWN`, in his words and firstmate's, task ids and repo URLs included. A turn (what he said, what came back) that names nothing carries on the project of the talk within 20 minutes of it, when that is one project. A message naming several projects is in each; a thread leaves out the paragraphs and points that name only others. A message naming more than four is treated as a fleet round-up and has no project tag.
- Unplaced exchanges go to firstmate (`project-asker.ts`): one note at a time through its inbox as Flyd's own note, at most 25 exchanges, cut short, and only while firstmate is up, spaced 15 minutes apart. Answers are kept by hash in `~/.flyd/view/project-tags.json`. Missing or unrecognized answers remain eligible for another request; an explicit Other answer is retained. Exchanges without a project tag appear under **Other**, the last row.
- His words in a thread go to that project's own conversation (`window:project-dir`), or to the live session when the source keeps none, led by `About the project "DIR"` and the last few lines of the thread (never shown to him); a message he points elsewhere with **To:** goes without that lead, so a follow-up means something there. A slash command goes as typed.

## The message box is Flyd's

- One model call routes each message (`flyd-desk.ts`).
- Above the box, **To:** says where a message goes (`ChatScope`, `runtime/chat-scope.ts`): Flyd's general chat unless he is in a project's thread or picks a project from its menu. It is sent with the message and goes exactly where it shows; Flyd's turn reads it as one line, so earlier talk about a project never makes a general message that project's.
- Shift+Up recalls his earlier messages, newest first, and Shift+Down walks back to the draft; plain Up and Down only move the caret.
- General questions get Flyd's own assistant turn (kept in `~/.flyd/view/asks`).
- Software/fleet work, slash commands and attached files go to firstmate's own `bin/fm-inbox.sh note`, each as a `desk-…` domain run through the command layer's FirstMate transport (his words, done_when, return contract; settled from receipts, transcript matching only a fallback). Only on firstmate's conversation.
- A screenshot goes with his words to whichever of them answers; Flyd reads it through a vision description kept with the ask.
- The **+** beside the box picks images and documents (the same types and limits as a drop or paste). In Flyd's own window documents go to Flyd's turn: saved under `~/.flyd/view/asks/files/<id>/`, read as text (`document-text.ts`: plain text as is, Word/RTF/OpenDocument via `textutil`, PDF via `pdftotext` when installed) and fenced in the prompt as outside content, so the turn is tainted like one that has read the web. The retrieval query, route and answer shape use his own words only; follow-ups replay an excerpt of each document.
- firstmate home is `~/Documents/firstmate` (`FLYD_FIRSTMATE_HOME` overrides; never the real one under vitest — `cli/src/lib/firstmate-home.ts`).
- "/" at the start of the box lists Claude Code's skills and commands (`commands.ts`, `/api/commands`); a sent command carries a hidden marker telling firstmate to run it.
- Pasted images: transcript ones are read back by line byte offset (`/api/image`); box ones are saved to firstmate's `data/inbox-images` and named in the note as `[image: /path]`.
- After push-to-talk speech stops, the box shows the Dynamic Island's spoke loader while the words are transcribed and until the reply starts (`page/script-composer.ts`); it follows the voice events and conversation updates, with no timer, and clears on a failed or cancelled recording, a send that did not go, or a reply shown elsewhere.
- Composer typing suggestions (Tab accepts, Escape dismisses, ↹ toggles): [flyd-composer-predictions.md](flyd-composer-predictions.md).

## Answers under a question

- A question's answer under it is only its own: Flyd's (shown whole when it fits a glance and its first line is at most 120 characters, else led by one such line and at most two more prose lines, a line asking him kept whole, while code, tables and the rest wait behind "more"; one with no prose is shown whole; `answerLead` in `cli/src/runtime/delivery.ts`; its card carries that lead line and, under it, only the line asking him), or firstmate's `fm-inbox.sh reply` from `state/inbox/.replies`.
- firstmate's reply is retold to George by Flyd's own model (`interpret.ts`): his question, the answer, the recent conversation and USER.md in; his question answered first in at most three short lines — the outcome, a decision only he can make, and links — in Flyd's voice. Shown through `deliver` (`cli/src/runtime/delivery.ts`): its first sentence fit to show leads, then at most two more lines, shaped by the reply (link, list, options). Cached in `~/.flyd/view/interpretations.json` (newest 20 kept), two model calls at a time. firstmate's narration under the busy dots is voiced too.
- firstmate's words sit behind "more"; the local digest leads until the interpretation lands; Flyd's whole briefing when none is coming. Otherwise it says it is waiting.

## Long-reply summaries (`summaries.ts`)

- Long replies lead with a summary of at most three lines, outcome first, how the work was done left out.
- Source order: the reply's own leading `» ` lines, else Claude Haiku → xAI Grok (from `CLAUDE_API_KEY`/`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or `XAI_API_KEY`/`~/.grok`). Cached in `~/.flyd/view/summaries.json`, newest 20 only.
- Until then: a local digest of the lead sentence and every point or paragraph. With no key: Flyd's whole briefing of the reply (stubs out, status card, every sentence).
- Sends reply text to that provider. `FLYD_VIEW_SUMMARIES=0` turns it off; `FLYD_SUMMARY_ALWAYS=1` compares.
- Routine replies (acks, status pings, a model's ROUTINE) are muted and never announced on the island.
- Replies with something to act on (code block, table, an instruction to him like "add these") open with the body shown under a model or digest summary (never under Flyd's whole briefing).

## The living line (`living.ts`)

- The busy dots stand alone, with no narrated step under them.
- While his newest message waits, one living line under it says what is happening to it, changed in place: "Queued" the moment he sends (or what his question asks about, e.g. "Looking up usb overdrive", when Flyd answers it), then what the message asks for with the project he named ("Fixing the island filter"), then firstmate's current step once its turn takes that note up by name.
- Never who took it or where it went; the island announces none of it. The answer replaces it.
